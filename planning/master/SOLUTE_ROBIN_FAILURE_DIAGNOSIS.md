## Aligned first-step evidence

The initial concentrations agree after reversing the Fortran order to a maximum absolute difference of `2.9911e-8`, consistent with conversion to the Fortran single-precision concentration state. The input schedule, 129-node coordinates, one-species order (S1), and physical request fields agree. This small representation difference is recorded as an input-state limit, not mistaken for exact bitwise equality.

Effective transport parameter translation agrees at the first interval. Both use dispersivity/effective dispersion `D=1 cm²/h`, water content `theta=0.43`, flux magnitude `|q|=0.43 cm/h`, and spacing `dx=0.015625 cm`. Thus pore velocity magnitude is `1 cm/h` and local `Pe=|v| dx / D = 0.015625`. Fortran reports signed `Pe2=-0.0078125` and upstream weight `wc=-0.00244140625`; those signs follow its reversed coordinate/velocity convention. Rust reports face diffusion coefficient `theta D/dx=27.52` and advective face contribution `q=0.43`, using its conservative face-flux/upwind stencil. No effective-dispersion translation mismatch is visible.

Storage and decay also map consistently: Rust linear mass derivative is `theta + rho Kd = 0.645`, decay is `0.2 h⁻¹`, and its first-step interior storage/reaction row-sum contribution is about `2.582`; Fortran reports retardation `1.5` and `g1=-0.129`, producing a comparable row sum. The first numerical divergence is already in the first solve assembly, before later time advancement. At the aligned midpoint, Fortran row `i=65` (Rust node 64) at its new-time level has `(B,D,E,F)=(-13.65259457, 30.10119629, -13.86759472, 1.96497154)`. Rust has `(lower,diagonal,upper,rhs)=(-27.95, 58.05201562, -27.52, 1.96529857)`. The two solvers assemble different spatial/time-weighted rows; raw tridiagonal entries should not be treated as identical weak-form coefficients without normalizing their respective row formulations. The RHS values are close, while the off-diagonal distribution differs substantially.

Boundary orientation is supported by both the authored case and source. `CASES.json` specifies inlet total flux with `C_in=1` and zero-gradient outlet. The Rust copied harness maps this to lower `TotalFlux(q*C_in)` and upper `AdvectiveOutflow` (`solute_verification_harness.rs`, `requested_study`). The Fortran deck maps `kTopCh=-1, cTop=1` to internal node `i=129`, where `x=0` and `vN=-0.43`; `kBotCh=-1, cBot=0` is at `i=1`, `x=-2`. `SOLUTE.FOR` `MatSet` applies the incoming advective concentration at the `kTopCh` endpoint and the outflow zero-gradient equation at `kBotCh`. The Fortran outlet row is `(B,D,E,F)=(0,-1,1,0)`; the Rust outlet retains its half-cell conservative balance row. At the inlet, Fortran has `(-13.65259457,15.15809822,0,1.32847661)` at `i=129`, while Rust node 0 has `(lower,diagonal,upper,rhs)=(0,29.24100781,-27.52,1.54355686)`. Boundary assembly therefore differs in representation and is a plausible contributor, alongside the interior stencil.

After the first solve, Rust minus reversed Fortran accepted concentration has mean `-9.92e-7`, range `-5.11e-5` to `+1.63e-6` (21 negative and 108 positive nodes). At the first failing profile, `0.0625 h`, the signed mean is `-1.6263e-5`, range `-2.0658e-4` to `+1.9296e-5` (51 negative and 78 positive nodes). The negative aggregate residual direction begins in the first interval, but local signs are mixed. These traced single-precision accepted values are not a substitute for the retained printer-format interval audit.

## Finding and limits

Supported: the retained effective dispersion and water-flux magnitudes agree; first-step storage/decay terms are consistent; the first observed cross-solver operator/state difference occurs during the initial assembly/solve, not only in later stepping. Fortran's local upstream weight is small and nonzero, while Rust uses a first-order upwind face flux. Fortran also uses a finite-element transport/time weighting and endpoint equations that differ from Rust's conservative finite-volume rows. The trace therefore falsifies a pure effective-D translation explanation and a later-stepping-only explanation.

Not isolated: this two-run comparison cannot assign the negative mean uniquely to `wc`, endpoint treatment, or the broader finite-element versus finite-volume discretization. Those effects enter the same assembled solve; no counterfactual weighting or boundary run was authorized. Raw matrix numbers also mix distinct row formulations. A discriminating follow-up should derive both first-step rows in a common weak-form/control-volume basis and then use a bounded task-local coefficient-only or single-factor counterfactual to separate interior weighting from endpoint assembly. Any extra solver launch requires a plan amendment.
